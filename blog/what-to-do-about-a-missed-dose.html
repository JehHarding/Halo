What Actually Happens
If You Miss a Dose

4 min read

A missed dose, after months of a perfect routine, tends to feel like a much bigger deal than it usually is. The right response depends entirely on the medication, and it's worth knowing before it happens, not while panicking after.

Why this is worth knowing in advance

The correct response to a missed dose genuinely varies. Some medications are fine given as soon as you remember. Others need the missed dose skipped entirely rather than doubled up. Guessing in the moment, stressed, is worse than having the answer ready beforehand.

The general rules, with the caveat that they're general

  • If it's close to the missed time: often fine to give as soon as remembered
  • If it's close to the next dose: often better to skip the missed one entirely rather than double up
  • Never double a dose to "catch up," unless a vet has specifically said that's fine for this medication
Worth doing now, not later: ask your vet or pharmacist directly, for each medication, "what should I do if I miss this one?" It takes one question and removes all the guessing later.

Why a one-off miss usually isn't a crisis

A single missed dose, handled sensibly, rarely undoes months of consistent treatment. The bigger risk is usually the panic response, doubling up out of guilt, which can cause more harm than the missed dose itself.

This guide is for information only and isn't veterinary advice. Always follow your own vet's specific instructions for a missed dose of a particular medication.

How to actually avoid the guessing

The far bigger win is not missing it in the first place, which comes down to a routine that doesn't rely on memory alone. Halo also makes a miss immediately obvious, an unchecked dose stands out clearly rather than getting lost in a busy day.
