Signs Your Bird Is Unwell
(They Hide It Even Better Than Cats)

4 min read

In the wild, a visibly sick bird is a target. That single evolutionary pressure means pet birds mask illness more completely than almost any other companion animal, cats included.

By the time a bird looks obviously unwell, fluffed up, sitting low, eyes half closed, they're very often already seriously ill. Birds instinctively hide weakness right up until they physically can't anymore, which makes catching the earlier, subtler signs genuinely important.

The early signs, before it's obvious

  • A change in droppings, colour, consistency or frequency, often the earliest measurable sign of a problem
  • Eating less, or eating differently, dropping more seed than usual, or leaving favourite foods untouched
  • Quieter than normal, less chatter, less singing, for a bird that's usually vocal
  • Sitting lower on the perch, or spending more time at the bottom of the cage
  • Reduced preening, or the opposite, over-preening a specific area

The signs that mean urgent, not soon

A fluffed-up posture held constantly, tail bobbing with each breath, sitting with eyes closed during the day, or any visible discharge from the eyes or nares are all signs of a bird in real distress. Birds hide illness so well that by the time these appear, veterinary attention is genuinely urgent, not something to monitor overnight.

Worth knowing: tail bobbing in time with breathing is a sign of real respiratory effort and distress. It's easy to miss if you're not specifically looking for it, and it's a reason to see a vet the same day.

Why droppings are worth checking daily

A healthy bird's droppings have a fairly consistent look, and changes there often show up before behaviour does. Colour, water content and frequency are all worth noticing as part of a normal daily routine, not just when something already seems wrong.

This guide is for information only and isn't veterinary advice. Every bird is different, always ask an avian vet what's normal for your own pet, and seek care quickly if anything changes.

Catching the change, not just the crisis

Because birds hide illness so effectively, the only real advantage an owner has is knowing their bird's normal well enough to notice the smallest shift.
