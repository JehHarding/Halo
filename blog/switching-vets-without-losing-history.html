Switching Vets Without
Losing the Medical History

4 min read

Moving to a new vet practice should mean the full history moves too. In practice, transfers are sometimes incomplete, delayed, or missing context that mattered. It's worth having your own copy regardless of how good the process is supposed to be.

What a formal transfer usually includes

Most practices will send core clinical records on request, diagnoses, treatments, vaccination history. What's less consistently included is the informal context, the notes a vet made mentally rather than officially, or the day-to-day pattern you've noticed that never made it into a formal record.

Why it's worth keeping your own copy anyway

  • Transfers can take time, and a new vet's first appointment often can't wait for paperwork
  • Your own daily observations were never going to be part of an official record in the first place
  • A record you control moves with you regardless of how any single practice handles transfers
Worth doing before the switch: ask your current practice for a summary while you're still a patient there, current medications, diagnoses, and key dates, rather than requesting it after you've already left.

What to actually bring to the first appointment

Current medications and doses, known conditions, recent vitals trends, and anything currently being monitored. A new vet forming an accurate picture quickly depends heavily on what you're able to bring, not just what eventually arrives from the old practice.

Why this is easier with a running record already

If Halo has already been tracking medications and vitals, switching vets doesn't mean losing anything, the record lives with you rather than with any particular practice, and the same Carer's Pack built for a sitter works just as well handed to a new vet.

Your own copy, always current

Halo keeps the full history on your device and in your own iCloud, independent of any single vet practice.
