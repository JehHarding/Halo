Building a Medication
Routine You Won't Forget

6 min read

Angel takes medication three times a day, every day, for the rest of her life. In the early days that felt genuinely impossible to keep straight. Years in, it hasn't slipped once, and it isn't because anyone's memory got better.

A medication routine that depends on remembering will fail eventually. Not because anyone's careless, but because life doesn't pause for a 7am dose: travel happens, sleep gets disrupted, houseguests change the shape of a normal day. The routines that actually hold up are the ones built to not rely on memory in the first place.

Anchor doses to things you already do

"Twelve o'clock" is easy to lose track of. "Right after I make coffee" isn't. Attaching a dose to an existing habit, brushing teeth, locking the door at night, feeding time, means the trigger is something that already happens reliably, instead of a bare number on a clock.

Build in a visual backup

Even with good habits, everyone has the moment of "wait, did I already do that?" A physical pill organiser, or anything you can glance at, answers the question instantly without relying on your memory of something that happened ten minutes ago in the middle of a normal, busy morning.

Write down what "normal" actually looks like

For every medication, it's worth having the basics written somewhere more permanent than your head:

  • The exact dose and what it's for
  • The time, and how much flexibility there actually is around it
  • What a missed dose means, and what to do about it
  • Anything it can't be given alongside, food, other medications, timing gaps
Tip: if two medications look similar, say so explicitly wherever you keep this written down. It's an easy, low-stakes-sounding mix-up that becomes a real problem with the wrong pet or the wrong dose.

Plan for someone else doing it

At some point, someone else will need to give a dose, a partner, a family member, a sitter, and they won't have years of built-up routine to fall back on. Whatever system you build should make sense to a person doing it for the very first time.
