What to Actually Tell
a New Vet, First Visit

4 min read

A new vet meeting a pet for the first time is starting from nothing, no history, no sense of what's normal for this specific animal. What you actually cover in that first conversation shapes how quickly they can catch up.

Start with the current state, not the full history

Current medications, doses, and any active conditions matter most in the first few minutes, before working backward into history. A new vet needs to know what's happening right now more urgently than everything that's happened over the years.

What's genuinely worth including

  • Every current medication, exact dose and timing
  • Known conditions and roughly when each was diagnosed
  • Recent vitals trends, not just the most recent single reading
  • Anything currently being monitored or watched
  • What's normal for this specific pet, quirks included
Worth doing: bring this written down, or ready to show on a phone, rather than relying on recalling it accurately in the room. It's easy to forget a detail under the mild pressure of a new appointment.

Why "what's normal for this pet" matters as much as medical facts

A vet meeting a pet for the first time has no baseline of their own yet. Telling them "she's always been a bit quieter than most cats" or "his breathing normally runs a little high" prevents a new vet from mistaking a genuine baseline quirk for a new symptom.
