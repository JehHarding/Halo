Flying With a Pet on
Medication: What to Check First

5 min read

A medication routine that runs smoothly at home gets more complicated the moment a flight, a time difference, and airline rules are added to it. Worth working through before booking, not the night before.

Time zones and dosing schedules

A twice-daily medication doesn't need to shift onto the destination's clock immediately, gradually adjusting the timing over the days either side of travel is usually easier on both of you than an abrupt jump. What matters most is keeping the actual spacing between doses roughly consistent, not chasing the exact original clock time.

Airline and documentation requirements

  • Check the specific airline's pet policy well in advance, requirements vary a lot between carriers
  • Carry medication in original, labelled packaging, not a repackaged organiser, for anything requiring documentation
  • A vet letter describing the condition and medications is worth having for international travel specifically
Worth doing early: some countries have specific import rules for medications, particularly controlled substances. This is worth checking weeks ahead, not assumed to be fine.

Storage during the actual journey

A refrigerated medication needs a proper cool bag for a flight, not just a normal carry item, cabin and cargo temperatures both vary more than a fridge does. Pack enough for delays too, not just the planned length of the trip.

What's worth having ready regardless of the destination
