Why Cats Hide
Illness So Well

4 min read

Angel has four heart conditions and has never once acted like it. That's not luck, and it's not her being especially resilient. It's what cats are built to do.

In the wild, a cat that looks sick is a target, both to predators, and to rivals looking to move in on their territory. Evolution didn't select for cats that announce weakness, it selected for the opposite, and that instinct hasn't gone anywhere just because a cat now lives on a sofa.

The instinct that never switched off

A cat in genuine pain or discomfort will very often act completely normal in front of you, purring, eating, seeming fine, right up until the underlying issue is serious enough that hiding it is no longer possible. By that point, whatever it is has usually been building for a while.

Why this trips up even attentive owners

This isn't about not paying attention. Even people who watch closely can miss it, because there's genuinely very little to see. The signs that exist are subtle by design: a slightly higher resting breathing rate, a little more time spent alone, a touch less interest in play.

Worth knowing: dogs tend to show illness more openly, limping, whining, obvious lethargy, which is part of why cat owners often feel like they're missing something dog owners aren't. You're not missing more, cats are just built to show less.

What actually works instead of watching harder

Since a cat won't reliably tell you something's wrong, the answer isn't more vigilance, it's measurement. A resting breathing rate, a weight, a note about behaviour, checked on a consistent schedule rather than only when something seems off, catches changes a cat is actively trying to hide.

This guide is for information only and isn't veterinary advice. If something feels off, trust that instinct and check with your vet, even without an obvious symptom to point to.
