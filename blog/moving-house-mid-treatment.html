Moving House With a
Pet Mid-Treatment

4 min read

A house move disrupts routine for every pet, unfamiliar boxes, new smells, a change in the whole environment. For a pet mid-treatment, the routine itself needs to survive that disruption too, not just the pet's mood.

What's worth sorting before the move

  • A new vet practice registered ahead of time, not found in a rush after something goes wrong
  • A full medication and dosage list ready to hand over, in case the current vet's transfer takes longer than expected
  • Enough medication on hand to cover any gap around the move itself

Keeping the routine stable on moving day

Moving day itself is genuinely one of the more stressful days a pet will have, and stress can affect appetite, which matters if medication needs to be given with food. Keep dose times as close to normal as realistically possible, and don't be surprised if appetite dips briefly around the move itself.

Worth doing: set up one small, familiar area in the new home immediately, food, water, bedding, the medication organiser, before unpacking anything else. Consistency in that one corner helps more than it sounds like it should.

Why the paperwork matters more during a move

A move is exactly the situation where important details get buried in boxes or forgotten in the chaos. Keeping the medical history somewhere independent of any single practice means it survives the move even if a formal records transfer takes a while.
