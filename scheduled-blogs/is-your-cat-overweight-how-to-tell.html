Is Your Cat Overweight?
How to Tell at Home

4 min read

Plenty of cats carry a little extra, and it is easy to stop noticing because the change is so gradual. You can check at home in a couple of minutes without any equipment.

Quick answer
Run your hands along your cat's side. You should feel the ribs under a thin layer, see a waist from above and a slight tuck behind the ribs from the side. If you have to press to find the ribs, or the belly swings low, talk to your vet about weight.

The rib check

Gently stroke your cat's side. You should be able to feel the ribs easily, like the back of your hand with a thin layer over it. If it feels more like the flat of your fist, there is probably extra covering. Long fur can hide this, so always use your hands as well as your eyes.

The waist and the belly

  • From above: your cat should narrow slightly behind the ribs. A straight or bulging outline suggests extra weight.
  • From the side: the belly should tuck up a little behind the ribs. A hanging belly pad is normal in some cats, but a belly that swings or sags is worth mentioning to your vet.

Why a small change matters

A cat who is only a few hundred grams over can already be at higher risk of joint problems, diabetes and breathing strain. Because that is a small amount on a small animal, the scales matter too. Weigh at the same time of day, on the same scales, and keep a record. How Often Should You Really Weigh Your Cat? covers how to do it.

What to do next

Please do not cut food suddenly. Rapid weight loss can harm a cat. Take your readings to your vet, who can set a safe target and a slow plan. The same applies in reverse: if your cat is losing weight without trying, that is a warning sign too.

Short on time? Do the rib check once a month, on the same day you weigh your cat. It takes less than a minute.
This guide is for information only and isn't veterinary advice. Ask your vet before changing your cat's diet.
