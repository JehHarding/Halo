What Living With a Cat
With a Heart Condition
Taught Me

4 min read

My cat Angel was diagnosed with a complex congenital heart condition in 2020. She has just turned seven. Here is what I have learned along the way.

Count the breaths, every time

The most useful thing I was ever asked to do was count Angel's breathing while she was asleep and relaxed. Her vets explained that a resting rate that creeps up can be an early warning, long before anything looks wrong. A number I could report beat "she seems a bit off".

Write it all down

Appointments are short and I am nervous in them. Having her weights, her medications and a log of how she has been meant I never had to rely on memory. When a new vet saw her, I could hand over the whole picture.

Keep every appointment in one place

Angel has far more appointments than a typical cat: check-ups, scans, bloods and follow-ups, often with different people. It is easy to lose track of what is booked, what is overdue and what the vet said last time. I keep them all in the calendar in Halo, so I can see at a glance what is coming up and never have to dig through old messages to find a date.

Quiet is not always fine

Cats hide how they feel. A cat who sleeps more, hides, or stops doing a small thing she always did is telling you something. I learned to trust that feeling and ask, even when it turned out to be nothing.

You are allowed to ask questions

I used to leave appointments with questions I had not dared to ask. Now I write them down beforehand: what changes should worry me, what is normal for her, and when I should call rather than wait.

Ordinary days matter most

Living with a serious condition is mostly ordinary days: medication, meals, naps in the sun. Getting the routine right is what lets the ordinary days keep happening.

This is one person's experience and isn't veterinary advice. Every pet's condition is different. Always follow your own vet's guidance.
