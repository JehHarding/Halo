Cat Not Eating:
When to Wait and
When to Call the Vet

4 min read

Few things worry a cat owner like a full bowl left untouched. Sometimes it is nothing. Sometimes it is the first sign that something is wrong, so it helps to know which is which.

Quick answer
A cat that skips one meal but is bright, moving about and drinking is usually fine to watch for a few hours. A cat that has not eaten for 24 hours, or is also vomiting, hiding or very quiet, needs a call to the vet the same day.

When it is probably nothing

Cats can be fussy. A new brand of food, a change of bowl, a hot day, a stressful visitor or a quiet day after the vet can all put a cat off one meal. If your cat is otherwise normal, try fresh food, a quieter spot and a slightly warmed portion, and see whether they come back to it.

When to call the vet

Cats are not built to go without food. After a day or two of not eating, some cats can develop serious liver problems, and cats who are overweight are at higher risk. Do not wait it out if you see any of these:

  • No food at all for 24 hours, or sooner for kittens, seniors and cats with a health condition
  • Vomiting, diarrhoea or drooling
  • Hiding, unusual quiet or no interest in anything
  • Struggling to chew or swallow, or pawing at the mouth
  • Any change in drinking, litter tray habits or breathing

If your cat is on medication

Some medicines can dull appetite, and some cats go off food because of an illness the medicine is treating. Do not stop or change a dose yourself. Call your vet, say what your cat is taking and when they last had it. The missed dose guide explains why it is better to ask than to guess.

What to note before you call

Your vet will ask the same few things, so write them down: when your cat last ate, what they were eating, whether they are drinking, any vomiting or diarrhoea, and their weight if you have it. A steady drop in weight is easy to miss, so a regular weigh-in matters. There is more on that in Gradual Weight Loss Is the Illness Sign Everyone Misses.

Never force-feed. Pushing food into a cat can scare them and cause choking. If they will not eat, that is the sign to phone the vet, not to try harder at home.
This guide is for information only and isn't veterinary advice. If your cat is unwell, contact your vet.
