Giving Liquid Medicine
to a Pet Without
the Mess

4 min read

Liquid medicine is often easier than tablets, until your pet decides otherwise. A calm routine and a few small habits make the whole thing quicker and kinder for both of you.

Quick answer
Use the syringe supplied, keep your pet's head level, aim into the cheek pouch rather than the back of the throat, give a little at a time and reward afterwards. If your pet keeps refusing, ask your vet about a different form.

Get ready first

  • Read the label and check what your vet said about shaking, storage and timing
  • Draw up the exact amount before you pick up your pet
  • Have a treat or a bit of food ready for afterwards
  • Choose a quiet spot and a towel if your pet fidgets

Where to aim

Keep your pet's head level or only slightly raised. Tipping it back makes swallowing harder and raises the chance of liquid going down the wrong way. Slide the tip of the syringe into the side of the mouth, into the cheek pouch behind the teeth, and give a little at a time, letting your pet swallow between squirts.

Make it easier next time

  • Do it at the same time each day so it becomes routine
  • Stay calm and quick. Pets pick up on tension.
  • Always finish with a treat, so the syringe means something good follows
  • Ask your vet whether the medicine can be flavoured. Our guide to compounded medications explains how that works.

If it keeps going wrong

Do not hide the dose in a full bowl of food unless your vet has said that is fine. If your pet refuses the food, they miss the whole dose. If your pet spits it out, do not guess whether to repeat it. Call your vet, who can tell you what to do for that medicine. If your cat is the difficult one, try How to Actually Give a Cat a Pill too.

Never force it. If your pet is struggling, coughing or gagging, stop, let them settle and try again later, or ask your vet for another option.
This guide is for information only and isn't veterinary advice. Always follow the directions on your pet's label and from your vet.
