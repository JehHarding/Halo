Dog Limping:
When to Rest and
When to Call the Vet

4 min read

A limp is your dog telling you something hurts. Many mild limps settle after a day or two, but some need prompt attention, and dogs are good at hiding pain.

Quick answer
A mild limp after exercise can be rested for 24 hours. See the vet if your dog will not put weight on a leg, the leg is swollen or at a strange angle, your dog cries out, or the limp lasts more than a day or two.

Start with rest

If your dog is limping slightly but is otherwise bright, keep them calm for a day: short lead walks to the toilet only, no running, jumping or stairs if possible. Look at the paw for thorns, cuts or a stuck stone, and gently feel the leg for heat or swelling.

Call the vet promptly if

  • Your dog will not put any weight on the leg
  • The leg is swollen, hot, dangling or at an odd angle
  • There was a fall, a collision or a trauma. Treat this as urgent.
  • Your dog yelps, snaps or will not let you touch the leg
  • The limp has lasted more than a day or two, or keeps coming back

Never give human painkillers

Ibuprofen and paracetamol can be dangerous or fatal for dogs. Only give pain relief prescribed by your vet for your own dog.

Older dogs and subtle signs

Stiffness after rest, slowing on walks and reluctance to jump can be early arthritis. Dogs hide pain well, so watch for small changes. Why Dogs Hide Pain Differently Than Cats and The Senior Dog Checklist will help you spot them.

Extra weight Every spare kilo puts more strain on joints. Keeping your dog lean is one of the best things you can do for them.
This guide is for information only and isn't veterinary advice. If your dog is in pain, contact your vet.
