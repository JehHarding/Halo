# Vet outreach — your own vet

Drafted 2026-08-13.

## Why this comes first, and alone

You do not yet have a single vet on record saying Halo is clinically sensible, and until you do, every other outreach email is weaker for it. Your own vet is the one place this doesn't have to be a cold email — you're a real client with a real patient, which no stranger's inbox can compete with.

This is shaped as a request for an opinion, not a pitch. Vets ignore pitches. They answer a client asking a genuine question.

Send this on its own, before anything else in the cardiologist or other-audiences lists.

---

## The email

**Subject:** A quick favour, and something I built for Angel

```
Hi [name],

You have looked after Angel through all four of her heart conditions, so
you know better than anyone how much of her care happens between
appointments.

I am a software developer, and over the last year I built an app to keep
on top of hers: medications, resting breathing rate, weight, and a record
of every appointment. It started as something just for us. It is now on
the App Store.

I am not asking you to recommend it. What I would really value is five
minutes of your honest opinion on two things. Whether it is clinically
sensible, and whether the summary it produces is something you would
actually find useful if a client handed it to you at a consult.

It is free, and it deliberately makes no diagnostic or treatment claims,
it is a record keeping tool and nothing more. If you would rather look at
a PDF than install anything, I can send you the summary it generates for
Angel.

Either way, thank you for everything you have done for her.

Jehane
myhaloapp.co.uk
```

## What to do with the answer

If it's positive, ask one follow-up: **"Would you be comfortable with me quoting that?"**

A single named vet saying Halo is sensible changes the tone of every email in the cardiologist and other-audiences outreach — it's the credibility that makes the rest of this work. See `CARDIOLOGY-EMAIL-LIST.md` and `OTHER-AUDIENCES-OUTREACH.md`.
