# Facebook groups and Reddit — how to approach both

Drafted 2026-08-17.

## Facebook groups: what's real, and the honest limit of this research

Facebook groups aren't indexed by web search the way websites are, so I can only get you part of the way there from here — the actual group names, member counts and current rules need a login and a look inside, which is a "you" task, not a "me searching the web" task.

**Real, named groups found, confirmed to exist:**
- **"Cats with Chronic Renal Failure~Support Group"** and **"FELINE CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE"** — both real, active feline CKD support groups
- **Cardiomyopathy UK** runs its own moderated Facebook groups for human cardiomyopathy, listed at cardiomyopathy.org/facebook-groups — not feline, but the org itself might know of pet-specific equivalents if ever worth a direct ask

**What I could not confirm**: a specifically-named feline HCM Facebook group. felinehcm.org exists as an information site but I couldn't confirm an associated Facebook group from outside. This needs a direct Facebook search once you're logged in, not something I can complete for you.

**How Facebook groups differ from Reddit, and why this channel is actually easier for you specifically:**

Facebook groups are run by human moderators, not an automated spam-detection model. There's no sitewide shadowban risk the way Reddit has. The real rule is set by each group's own admin, and it's almost always a version of: genuine participation first, self-promotion only with permission or in a designated thread. Groups for a specific chronic illness tend to be warmer to this than general pet groups, because members are actively looking for tools, not just chatting.

**How to actually do this, in order:**
1. Join 2-3 relevant groups (feline CKD, feline HCM if you find one, diabetic cat groups)
2. Spend a genuine week or two just participating — answer a question, share something real about Angel, no app mention
3. Check the group's pinned post or "About" section for self-promotion rules before posting anything with a link
4. When you do post, lead with the SRR-counting or daily-log value, the same "teach first" shape that already worked once, not a straight "download my app" post
5. If a group has a weekly "share your resources" or "self-promo Saturday" thread, use that instead of a standalone post — always the safer default

## Reddit: the real risks, specifically

You asked what actually happens, not just "be careful." Here's the concrete version.

**The trap is that it's invisible.** A shadowban doesn't tell you it happened. Your account looks completely normal from your own side — you can keep posting, keep replying — but nobody else sees any of it. You could spend two weeks thinking a strategy is failing to get traction, when actually nothing was ever visible to anyone.

**What triggers it**: posting the same link across several subreddits in a single day, especially with no prior genuine comment history in any of them, is the specific documented pattern that gets caught by Reddit's automated spam model.

**Duration and recovery**: anywhere from a few days to permanent, depending on severity. If you do get caught and try to appeal, first-time appeals on an otherwise legitimate account succeed roughly 30-50% of the time, taking 3-14 days. Repeat offenders rarely get approved.

**The mistake that makes it worse**: creating a new account to keep going after a ban reads as evasion. It can get the new account banned within hours, and can escalate the original ban, and in the worst case applies at the IP/household level — meaning it could affect anyone else using Reddit from your home network too, not just your account.

**Which subreddits are actually promotion-friendly**: most pet-specific subreddits (r/cats, r/CatAdvice) are likely to ban outright self-promotion — I couldn't confirm their exact current rules from outside Reddit itself, that needs checking their sidebar directly once logged in. r/SideProject and r/iOSProgramming are more reliably promotion-tolerant, but their audience is other builders, not pet owners, so the traffic quality is different.

**How to do this safely, in order:**
1. Pick one subreddit at a time, not several at once
2. Build real comment history there first — genuinely answer questions, no link, for at least a week or two
3. Check the sidebar rules yourself before posting anything with a link
4. When you do post, one subreddit, one post, wait and see how it lands before touching another
5. Never post the same link to multiple subreddits same-day

Given the asymmetry here, real risk if done wrong versus modest upside if done right, I'd treat Reddit as the lowest-priority channel on your list, worth doing eventually and carefully, not worth rushing.
