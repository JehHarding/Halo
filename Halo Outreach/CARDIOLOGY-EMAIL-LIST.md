# Cardiologist outreach — email and full contact list

Compiled 2026-08-16. Self-contained: the draft email, sending rules, and the full list are all in this one document.

**Send your own vet's email first** (`VET-OUTREACH.md`), before any of these. One vet saying Halo is clinically sensible changes how every email below reads.

**One honest line on sourcing, then straight into it.** UK entries are 16 practices researched directly (real websites, no guessed emails). US, Canada and international entries further down are pulled from a single third-party directory (a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel health charity's cardiologist list), not re-verified address by address. That directory is built for owners finding a cardiologist for their own pet, so a few addresses may be out of date or personal rather than professional-facing. Expect some bounces — normal for a list this size from a single source, not a sign anything's wrong.

## The email

**Subject:** A digital alternative to the SRR monitoring booklet

```
Dear [name or Cardiology Service],

You send owners of newly diagnosed heart failure patients home with a
booklet to record sleeping respiratory rate, and ask them to bring it to
the recheck. In practice a lot of those booklets get lost, filled in from
memory the night before, or abandoned after a fortnight.

I am the owner of a cat with four heart conditions, and I am also a
software developer. I built Halo to solve that problem for myself. It
records SRR, medications and weight, charts the trend over time, and
exports a one page summary the owner can bring in or email ahead of a
recheck.

I am not asking you to endorse or recommend anything. I would like to know
whether a cardiologist would find that summary useful, and whether there
is anything in it you would want changed before it were ever put in front
of your clients. If the answer is that it is not useful, that is genuinely
worth me knowing.

It is free, there is no account or sign up, and it makes no diagnostic or
treatment claims. I am happy to send a sample PDF rather than ask you to
install anything.

Kind regards,
Jehane Harding
Halo, myhaloapp.co.uk
hello@myhaloapp.co.uk
```

**The argument behind it, in one paragraph.** Standard cardiology practice after a heart failure diagnosis is to send the owner home to record sleeping respiratory rate daily, on paper, in a booklet the practice provides. Published owner guidance already says a diary or a computer database is an acceptable alternative. So Halo isn't asking anyone to change protocol or adopt something new — it's the alternative their own guidance already permits, built properly. A much smaller thing to say yes to than a new product.

**A terminology point.** Halo's interface already says "Respiratory Rate" throughout, so you can write to a cardiologist without worrying about the app using the wrong language. The one real nuance: cardiology tends to specify *sleeping* respiratory rate rather than *resting*, since sleeping is less prone to being inflated by stress. Worth asking about in the first email rather than something to fix beforehand — it shows you care about the distinction.

## If someone replies positively

Don't immediately ask for promotion. Ask in this order:

1. "Would you be comfortable with me quoting that?"
2. "Is there anything you would want changed?" Then actually change it, and tell them you did. This is the step that turns a polite reply into a relationship.
3. Only then: "Would you be willing to mention it on your home monitoring page, or to clients you send home with a booklet?"

Offer free Halo Plus codes for their clients at step 3, not before — App Store Connect can generate these at no cost.

## Rules for sending

- **Personalise the first line every time.** A visibly templated email to a specialist reads as spam and burns the contact permanently.
- **Send in small batches, a week apart.** Not the whole list at once — you want to learn from early replies and adjust before the rest go out.
- **Never BCC a group.**
- **Get the address from the practice's own contact page where one isn't given below.** Don't guess a format.
- **Send from hello@myhaloapp.co.uk**, not a personal address.

**Compliance, briefly.** Under UK PECR, unsolicited marketing email to a corporate subscriber (a limited company or LLP) is allowed without prior consent; sole traders and ordinary partnerships count as individual subscribers and aren't covered the same way, and plenty of independent vet practices are partnerships. Two things keep this comfortable regardless: these emails ask for a professional opinion rather than solicit a sale, and Halo is free. Always sign with a real name and address, which the template above already does. Not legal advice — worth a proper look if this scales past a few dozen personalised emails.
