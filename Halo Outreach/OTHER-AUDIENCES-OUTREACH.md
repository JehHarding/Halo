# Beyond cardiology, and the Carer's Pack angle

Drafted 2026-08-16.

## 1. Who else would want the daily log, weight and respiratory rate trio

The useful finding here: **you probably don't need a second cold-email list.** The same multi-specialist referral hospitals already on the cardiology list — NDSR, Davies, Willows, RVC, the university hospitals — run internal medicine, renal and weight clinics under the same roof as their cardiology service. Widening the pitch inside the emails you're already sending covers most of this for free.

Where it's worth a mention or a small addition:

**Internal medicine / chronic kidney disease.** CKD is managed almost identically to heart failure from a monitoring standpoint: weight trend and a daily log are the two things a nephrologist actually wants back from an owner between rechecks. RVC's nephrology service (part of the same RVC Queen Mother Hospital already on your list) is the clearest UK example — worth namechecking CKD specifically when you write to RVC, since it's a second, independent reason for them to care beyond cardiology.

**Weight management / obesity referral clinics.** Two real UK ones, both legitimate and both explicitly built around weight trend as the whole point of the service:
- **Royal Canin Weight Management Clinic, University of Liverpool** — the first specialist weight-loss clinic for pets anywhere, run by Prof. Alex German. Referral only, via the pet's own vet.
- **QVSH Obesity Clinic, Cambridge** — referrals via hospital@vet.cam.ac.uk (this is a vet-to-vet referral address, not an owner contact, but useful if you want to reach the clinic through your own vet or reference it directly).

Both are a good "if it's useful for weight management too" line in an email to Cambridge or a general email to Liverpool, not a reason to build a whole separate outreach track.

**Diabetic patients.** Same logic as CKD — home glucose curves plus weight plus a log is standard diabetic cat/dog management. No dedicated diabetes referral network the way cardiology or weight has one; this is more a case for the app's marketing copy ("also useful for diabetes, CKD and any chronic condition needing a daily log") than a distinct outreach list.

**Senior/geriatric wellness clinics.** Many first-opinion practices run a "senior pet clinic" nurse-led service that already asks owners to track weight between visits. Not really specialist-level enough to be worth researching and listing individually — this is more of a blog-post/social-content angle than an email list.

**Bottom line:** add one sentence to the cardiology emails when you're writing to a multi-specialty hospital ("this is equally useful for internal medicine and weight management follow-ups, not just cardiology"), and don't build a second target list for this.

## 2. The Carer's Pack — catteries and kennels

This is the better new idea of the two, and the evidence backs it up: **UK boarding kennels and catteries already advertise "we administer medication" as a selling point.** That's the exact gap the Carer's Pack fills — a clear, printable/shareable one-pager of what's due when, instead of a scrawled note handed over at drop-off.

### The higher-leverage move: the trade press, not just individual kennels

**Kennel & Cattery Management** is the UK trade magazine for boarding professionals, published since 1983, read by the exact audience you want. One mention or a short feature there reaches far more catteries than any number of individual cold emails — the same "one relationship, many clinics" logic as the peripatetic cardiologists from the vet list. I couldn't pull a direct editor email in this pass; their contact page is at kennelandcattery.com — worth a two-minute look before emailing the full list above, since it could replace a chunk of the one-by-one work. There's also a UK Alliance of Catteries and Kennels and an Association of Dog Boarders, both worth a look for the same reason.

### Suggested approach

Don't pitch this as "please recommend our app to your clients." Pitch it as **useful to the cattery's own operations**, which is genuinely true: fewer dosing mistakes, less liability for them, a document owners can hand over rather than a hurried verbal briefing. That's a different, easier ask than the vet emails — you're not asking for a clinical endorsement, you're offering to make their intake process safer.

**General draft**, for everyone on the list except Freer Farm:

**Subject:** A clearer way for your boarders' owners to hand over medication instructions

```
Hi [name],

I noticed on your website that you're happy to administer medication for
boarding pets, which a lot of catteries and kennels aren't. I imagine that
still means relying on a handwritten note or a rushed conversation at
drop-off to get the dosing right.

I built Halo, a free app for pet owners managing medication, vet visits
and health records. One of its features is a "Carer's Pack": a one-tap PDF
summary of exactly what a pet is on, when, and any notes from the owner,
that they can hand you (or email ahead) instead of a scrawled note.

I'm not asking you to recommend the app to your clients, I'd just like to
know whether that's something that would actually make your intake
process easier, or whether I'm missing something about how you currently
handle this. Happy to send you a sample PDF rather than ask you to install
anything.

Kind regards,
Jehane
Halo, myhaloapp.co.uk
```

Same rules as the vet outreach apply: personalise the opener with something specific from their own site, send in small batches, use hello@myhaloapp.co.uk to send. Where only a phone number is listed above, that's genuinely all that business publishes — call, or check their site for a contact form, rather than guessing an email format.
